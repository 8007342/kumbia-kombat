# Swing duet research (2026-11-05)

For the pool-garden level (`plan/levels/pool-garden.md`, `plan/duet-foundations.md`): two adult women, fast swing, dancing with each other, then Mācron answers on four zones with no chords.

**How far to trust this.** Every page fetch failed with a connection reset, apart from one GitHub wiki, so nearly all of this rests on search-result excerpts, as in the Gatekeeper pass. I watched no footage and read no book or full paper. A swing teacher should review it before art starts.

Labels: **[S]** a source says so (refs at the end). **[I]** my inference or arithmetic. **[M]** my background knowledge, not confirmed in this pass. Every floor pattern and key pose in §3 is [I]/[M] unless a [S] ref is attached.

## 1. Style

**Recommendation [I]: Savoy-style Lindy Hop with its Charleston family as the main vocabulary, plus solo jazz for breaks.** It is the only candidate that has place swaps, open and closed work, a built-in solo break, and a working range up to the tempos wanted. Charleston is not a rival choice: Manning and other Savoy dancers saw themselves as doing Charleston steps within the Lindy [S: R2].

| Style | Sources say | Fit for two sprites on a floor plane [I] | Verdict [I] |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lindy Hop | Partner dance with 8- and 6-count patterns, breakaway and swing out [S: R1, R14, R17] | Long, readable floor pattern, partners separate and swap ends | **Primary** |
| Charleston (solo, side-by-side, tandem, hand-to-hand) | 8-count basic, solo or partnered; 1930s form keeps knees closer than the 1920s form [S: R13, R19, R20] | Big limb shapes, least overlap. Dancers lean on it as tempo climbs [S: R23] | **Core of Lindy vocabulary** |
| Balboa | Close embrace, "smooth, tight footwork", fast music, from 1930s Southern California [S: R13, R24, R25] | Fastest, but little floor pattern and bodies pressed together | Rejected as primary |
| Collegiate Shag | Double-rhythm basic of 6 counts; about 180-200+ BPM. Sources conflict on 6 vs 8 counts [S: R13, R26, R27] | Fast and bouncy, a small hoppy pattern with less to swap | Rejected; possible cameo |
| East Coast Swing | Based on Lindy Hop; 6-count; tempo standard about 136-144 BPM [S: R28, R29] | Too slow as usually specified, thin on place changes | Rejected |
| Rock'n'Roll (acrobatic) | Competition sport, 48-52 bars/min, which is 192-208 BPM [S: R30, R31] | Right speed, but built on lifts and throws; ill-fitted to the level's tone | Rejected |

### History and respect

| Point | Status |
|---|---|
| Lindy Hop was created by Black dancers in Harlem and is tied to the Savoy Ballroom. Its exact origin is contested: Wikipedia lists several possible roots rather than one inventor | [S: R1, R4, R10, R12] |
| The breakaway came first: a syncopated two-step where the couple break apart for improvised solos. George "Shorty" Snowden is credited with the Lindy name, around 1927-28 (accounts differ) | [S: R1, R4] |
| Frankie Manning was chief choreographer and a lead dancer for Whitey's Lindy Hoppers, is credited with the first Lindy aerial (1935) and a low, stretched swing out, and taught worldwide after the 1980s revival. He died in 2009, aged 95 | [S: R2, R9] |
| Norma Miller, the "Queen of Swing", won a Savoy Lindy contest in 1934 with Sonny Ashby and took the dance to film, Broadway and Europe with Whitey's | [S: R3, R11] |
| White dancers in Hollywood films helped popularise it; later scene discussion stresses crediting Black American originators | [S: R4, R10] |
| The *A Day at the Races* dance sequence could be cut for segregated venues; in *Hellzapoppin'* several of the dancers are costumed as maids and a chef | [S: R34, R36]. Do not copy those costumes [I] |

Rules for the level [I]:
- Use real step names only, and credit them in the level's notes; "Shorty George" is a person's signature step [S: R6].
- No mugging, bulging eyes or "wild" jitterbug caricature, a known trap in period film. Dancers are skilled and in control.
- Solo jazz is not decoration: Suzie Q, Shim Sham, Tranky Doo come from Black vernacular and tap lineages, with named creators where known [S: R38, R39].
- Consider a Black swing reviewer as the "reviewer still to find" and raise the casting of the two women with the owner. The Frankie Manning Foundation may be a route, though I found no consulting offer [S: R7, R8].

### Same-sex partnering and switching

| Point | Status |
|---|---|
| Lindy has traditionally paired a male lead with a female follow, but many dancers learn both ("switch dancing"); women leading is "completely normal" in many scenes, and switching is called everyday practice | [S: R15, R16] (teaching sites, not surveys) |
| Classes teach how to signal a change mid-dance; contests such as Flying Home's Switch Mix and Match say "lead, follow, and anything in between" | [S: R18, R21] |
| ILHC Strictly defines a couple as one leader and one follower, and lets a dancer enter one division per role; I saw no gender rule | [S: R22] |
| Etiquette blog: asking to dance is not asking to switch. Dogpossum's blog (opinion, date unchecked) says women leads are rare among major-event winners | [S: R43, R44]. So women trading roles is real and accepted, and not yet common at the very top |
| Historic: same-sex pairs were common when partners were short, and one guide says women led each other in wartime. Neither source is about the Savoy, and I could not pin the wartime claim to a URL | [S: R42] partly. **Do not state as Savoy history** |
| A WRRC page says Finland's federation allows female-female couples, but only male-female couples may represent it internationally under WRRC rules. Date not checked; another reason to avoid that style | [S: R45] |

Game use [I]: their trade reads best at a hand change or a swap at a phrase boundary. I found no written technique for the physical cue, so that goes to the reviewer.

**Swing ratio.** Friberg & Sundström measured ride cymbals on recordings: as high as 3.5:1 slow, 1:1 fast; 2:1 only at one tempo region; the short note stays about 100 ms from medium to fast tempo. Soloists differ from drummers below 160 BPM [S: R50, R51, R52]. A later paper links downbeat delays to swing feel [S: R53, title only].

Worked from those figures [I]: A = fixed 100 ms short note. B = straight line 3.5 at 100 BPM to 1 at 300 BPM, from a secondary citation [S: R54]. They differ by about 0.25 at most, so these are starting values to tune by ear.

| BPM | Beat ms | Ratio A | Ratio B | Offbeat at (fraction of beat, A) | 8 counts | 32 bars |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 150 | 400 | 3.0 | 2.9 | .75 | 3.20 s | 51.2 s |
| 180 | 333 | 2.3 | 2.5 | .70 | 2.67 s | 42.7 s |
| 200 | 300 | 2.0 | 2.25 | .67 | 2.40 s | 38.4 s |
| 220 | 273 | 1.7 | 2.0 | .63 | 2.18 s | 34.9 s |
| 240 | 250 | 1.5 | 1.75 | .60 | 2.00 s | 32.0 s |

Game consequence [I]: the music generator should vary swing by tempo. The player cannot be graded on it: at 200 BPM a swung and a straight offbeat differ by about 50 ms (17% of a beat), well inside the ±180 ms good window. Offbeat taps should be spaced from neighbours by at least one beat at top tempos.

**Counts.** 8-count: `1-2, 3-a-4, 5-6, 7-a-8`. 6-count: `1-2, 3-a-4, 5-a-6` [S: R14]. At speed a triple can become a single step, or a kick-step [S: R23, R28].

**Grid problem [I].** A call is 8 beats, and the response 8 more (`plan/dance-battle-choreography.md`). 6-count moves do not fit 8- or 16-beat blocks: within 32 beats, 6s and 8s only fill as eight × 4 or six × 4 + eight × 1. Options: use 8-counts in calls and keep 6-counts for response-time dancing, or let four 6-counts span 24 beats while the chart stays on its own grid, since 6-count patterns commonly run across the 8-count phrase in real dancing [M].

## 3. Vocabulary

A and B are the two dancers. Roles trade, so names are not tied to a person. Assumption [I]: the slot lies on a floor diagonal of about 30-45°, so partners separate on screen and change depth together. A slot straight at the camera hides one dancer. Lindy Hop's slot is [M]; circles usually go clockwise [S: R55].

"Tier" is my proposed first appearance (T0-T4). "Sprite" is risk with two separate sprites with front, rear, side and three-quarter views.

**Swing out budget [I].** It is the dance's defining move [S: R17], so a Lindy level without it would look wrong. Its middle counts (3-5) are two bodies in a hold, rotating. Plan to draw that moment as **one joined two-person sprite** of about 3 drawings, swapping to the separate sprites on the open counts. Do the same for any closed hold and for the tied-foot case in `plan/duet-foundations.md`.

**Avoid [I].**
- All aerials: around-the-back, candlestick, barrel roll, basket [S: names R17]. They are real, Manning's own innovation, but they need a lift, views the rig lacks, and suit neither the tone nor a credible read at this size.
- Waist wrap (belt slide), cuddle (wrap-in), sweetheart and dips [S: names R17]. Occlusion, plus a romantic read the owner hasn't decided on (the bible proposes friends or sisters).
- Sustained free spins: four views cannot show them. Use one quick transit, and never fake them by flipping the image (Gatekeeper rule).
- High kicks while the camera is low: the bible rules out shots from robot height. Keep kicks at about knee to mid-thigh height, and check wardrobe.

Size note [I]: the depth band y 0.42-0.58 changes size by 60%. One couple's span covers only part of that, so the **pair centre** has to walk the depth over several moves. A floor track should describe the pair centre, then each dancer's offset from it.

## 4. Adornments

Repo note: `dance_grammar.dart` has **accents** (`everyBeats`, `atBeat`, `minTier`, `clip`) and `responseByTier`; the word "adornment" is not in it. `adornments` in score files are something else: the **player's** optional up/down/left/right flourishes (`spark`, `dip`, `slide`, `shine`, quantised to 0.25 beat, `garden_page.dart`). So a dancer's styling belongs in accents and response clips, and "shine" already has a meaning in the code.

**Signalling.** Sources say a break cannot be led once contact is gone, and a set break should start only if the partner knows it [S: R65]. Early accounts have the lead flinging the partner out, then improvising [S: R1]. Jam-circle dancers negotiate turns visually [S: R67]. I found **no written rule** on how long a shine lasts or how to signal one.

Game rules [I]:
- A dancer's kick looks like a foot call. Keep the existing check (`requireFrames` refuses a call accent in a groove clip) and extend it by eye: **adornments on the shoulders, head, hips and swivels during calls; big kicks only on the bridge while no call is live**, or on the limb being called.
- Signal a shine with the hand release on count 8 and a look to the partner. That's cheap, and it reads from a distance.

## 5. Phrasing and escalation

**Structure.** 32 bars, usually AABA, with B contrasting [S: R68, R69]. Careful with the arithmetic [I]: 1 eight-count = 2 bars; 4 eight-counts = 8 bars = **one A section**; a chorus = 16 eight-counts = 128 beats. One search excerpt implied four eight-counts make a chorus, which is wrong on that arithmetic. Jam-circle research reports a phrase of 4×8 beats, structured aaba or aaab, with couples often going over or under it [S: R67]. Twelve-bar blues would be 6 eight-counts [I]. Existing scores are already 32 bars in 4 sections of 8 (`bar_swag.json`), so AABA maps directly: Invitation/Answer/Lift/Approval as A/A/B/A [I].

**Jam circles.** Length not fixed in advance; the next couple negotiates the floor visually among music, spectators and the current couple; three spatial patterns exist but my excerpt cut off before naming them [S: R67]. Useful for the Out and for the crowd's clap behaviour, once the full paper is read. **Clapping conventions not verified.**

**Shim Sham convention.** After the routine, dancers grab a partner and Lindy for the rest of the song [S: R39]. A scored route from solo, to partnered, to solo mirrors this [I].

**Real escalation finding [S→I].** Contest spotlights go slower then faster (160-170, then 200-230) [S: R48]. As tempo rises dancers **simplify footwork** and turn to Charleston [S: R23]. So complexity does not climb straight with tempo. In the game, tap density should peak below top tempo, and the fastest tier should get bigger floor moves and sparser, cleaner taps.

Proposed ladder [I], all tempos to test on a phone (existing tier steps are only 3%; this is untested):

| Tier | BPM | Dance | Connection and swaps | Chart shape |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 150 | Side-by-side and solo Charleston, Sugar push | Hip or no contact, no swaps | One limb family, on the beat, rests |
| 1 | 160 | Hand-to-hand, Tandem, Send out, Side pass, Shorty George | One swap per section | Two limbs, first offbeat |
| 2 | 170 | Swing out arrives, She Goes He Goes, Tuck turn, Swivels; bridge breakaway | A swap per A section, first bridge solo | Four limbs, syncopation |
| 3 | 185 | Combinations: Swing out into Tandem, Eggbeater, Texas Tommy; solo trading on bridge | Role trade visible | **Densest rhythm** |
| 4 | 200 | Long Swing out runs, fast Charleston, big drifting depth, Drop Boogie/Fall off the Log breaks | Whole floor used | Sparse, big shapes at 300 ms per beat |

**Limb truth [I].** A facing pair is mirrored: one dancer's left foot is on the viewer's right. Tandem has both on the same foot and facing the same way, so its limbs are the clearest. Calls must come from the cue layer, with a dancer's limb only as flavour. Decide which dancer "holds" each phrase.
