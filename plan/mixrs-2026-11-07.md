# Mixing in Rust, 2026-11-07

Results in full: `tool/music/mixrs/README.md`.

## Facts

- The stall came from memory. Whole-take separation added 2.8 GB per worker and whole-take EQ 1.2 GB.
- One level, bar: 3446 MB and 44.8 s before, 1529 MB and 37.6 s with MUSIC's cap, 185 MB and 16.2 s with mixrs. Largest process, at one worker.
- All five levels: 162.9 s and 1529 MB with the cap, 66.2 s and 240 MB with mixrs.
- Whole process tree per level with mixrs: 266 to 313 MB. The target was 300, so pool-garden misses by 13 MB. mixrs alone peaks at about 180 MB.
- Same decisions as the Python on 7 level builds, and the same audio before encoding to -144 dB or better, except garden/high.
- `mix.py verify` has 0 failures, and `--self-test` passes, on both engines.

## What went wrong along the way

- My first baseline used the committed config, which has no layered tiers, so it missed the real cause. MUSIC's note pointed at it.
- My first Rust EQ kept the whole-take FFT and took 738 MB. Measuring caught it. It's now blocks, at -151 dB against the whole-take result.
- `verify_layers` crashed the self-test on an 8-bar loop before this work. Fixed here.
- Numeric tolerances in the comparison were set after the first run, and one was widened to fit a 0.02 ms gap. Decisions were always required to be equal.

## For the owner's ears, or a decision

- ffmpeg's AAC output moves by about -25 dB in a null test from last-bit changes in its input, so these AAC files don't null against the Python's. Opus nulls to about -78 dB, and the audio before encoding to about -147 dB. It may not be audible, and nobody here can hear it.
- Garden/high masters at a -1.5 dB ceiling with mixrs and -1.9 dB in Python, for that reason. Both pass.
- Proposal: rebuild assets once with mixrs and listen to garden/high.
