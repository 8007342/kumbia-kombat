## Dress code, for every level, old and new

The owner: "the majority of the party people are dressed up nicely, the Pool room has some people in hoodies and baseball caps, please note they're at an elegant party at a huge mansion, so everyone should be dressed up, not necessarily expensive or night dresses, but everyone should be classying it up for the room they're in." So no hoodies, baseball caps or T-shirts. Smart casual or better, suited to each room: guayaberas and linen on the lawn, suits and dresses indoors, waistcoats at the billiards table.

## Room life at full party

The owner: "Full party should also have the chandeliers and elegant paintings on the wall doing gently invisible motions, like tapping their foot or snapping their fingers, even the paintings got engaged ;) or a sculpture by the corner." This is a reusable layer for any room: subtle, almost subliminal motion that only starts at the top tiers. Examples: a portrait's foot taps, a painted gentleman snaps his fingers, a marble bust nods, chandeliers sway on the beat. Keep it within the motion-invisibility lessons, so it's felt more than seen.

## 6. El Gran Comedor, `comedor`: happy polka

- **Room:** the mansion's long dining hall. A banquet table runs across the room behind the dance floor, with diners eating supper: soup, candelabra, silver.
  - Gilt-framed portraits and still lifes on damask walls, two chandeliers, and a marble sculpture in a corner niche.
  - Warm candlelight with wine-red and cream.
- **Crowd as meter:** at a low tier the diners eat and ignore him. As the party rises they stop eating, turn, and start clapping, and at the top they **rise and lift their glasses**. The portraits, the sculpture and the chandeliers join in subtly at full party.
- **Music:** a **norteño polka**, `comedor_polka` "Sobremesa". Accordion lead, bajo sexto, tololoche or tuba bass on the oom, snare on the pah. Bright and bouncy, about 120, 128 and 136 BPM, in a major key.
- **Opponent: Don Charolas** ("Mr. Trays"), the head waiter. He. Short and round-cheeked, with slick centre-parted hair and a tiny moustache, in a white waiter's jacket, black bow tie and a long white apron. He balances a silver tray (or a teetering tower of them) on one hand while he polkas, never spilling a drop.
  - **Staff:** two or three junior waiters behind him hop the polka with trays, and Mācron "dances his way through the staff".
  - **Hardest combo:** `LA TORRE DE CHAROLAS` (the tower of trays).
- **Metric:** `Gusto`.
- **Outro:**
  1. Don Charolas spins to a stop and presents a silver cloche.
  2. In the close-up he lifts it: under it is a tiny can of motor oil with a birthday candle, a dessert for a robot. Mācron sips it happily. The title is **QUÉ / BANQUETE**.
  3. In the wide shot, all the diners rise and lift their glasses ("¡Salud!"). The waiters form a line and Mācron hops the polka between them out through the service door toward the salon.

## 8. La Galería, `galeria`: tense, Celtic reel

- **Room:** a long portrait gallery in the mansion. A polished marble floor made for footwork, tall windows, marble statues on plinths, and a long carpet runner down the middle. Cool silver moonlight with warm picture lights over each painting.
- **Opponent: Siobhán and Las Tréboles** ("the Clovers"): a line of five Irish step dancers who dance as one. Original costumes, not a copy of any real show: dark green velvet with silver embroidery, rigid upright arms, and fast, precise, heavy feet. Siobhán, the lead, is in the centre. She.
  - The line moves in perfect unison, and the art and choreography should sell "as one": one set of frames, offset by a frame or two at most.
  - **Hardest combo:** `EL MURO DE TACONES` (the wall of heels).
- **Music:** a **Celtic reel**, `galeria_reel` "Trébol". Fiddle, tin whistle, uilleann-pipe drone, bodhrán, and hard-shoe percussion as part of the groove. Driving and tense, about 112, 120 and 128 BPM (a reel's crotchet pulse), in a modal minor (Dorian).
- **Chart:** foot-heavy, fast and relentless, with feet on most notes and hands rare. It's the tense counterweight.
- **Metric:** `Drive`.
- **Outro:**
  1. The line freezes, then splits in two and raises its arms into an arch.
  2. The close-up is on Mācron's tiny feet doing one perfect double tap, then his proud face. The title is **QUÉ / ZAPATEO**.
  3. He walks under the arch, and Siobhán gives one approving nod.

## 9. La Terraza, `terraza`: happy, samba

- **Room:** the mansion's rooftop terrace at midnight. Strings of festoon lights, potted palms, a balustrade, the city lights below, and fireworks bursting in the sky as the party rises. The guests are in elegant summer evening wear.
- **Opponent: Tía Purpurina** ("Auntie Glitter"). She. A joyful older lady in a sequinned gold cocktail dress, a feather boa and big earrings, with unstoppable samba hips and a laugh you can see. She's the life of every party.
  - **Hardest combo:** `LA LLUVIA DE CONFETI`.
- **Music:** **samba batucada**, `terraza_samba` "Purpurina". Surdo, caixa, tamborim, agogô, a whistle call, cavaquinho and a brass lift. About 96, 104 and 112 BPM, joyful and in a major key.
- **Metric:** `Joy`.
- **Outro:**
  1. She throws a handful of confetti.
  2. In the close-up she plants a big lipstick kiss mark on Mācron's cheek panel, and his antenna sparks like a sparkler. The title is **QUÉ / CHISPA**.
  3. Fireworks fill the sky and Mācron dances off toward the stairs.

## Later levels: design only now, built in a later batch

### 10. The restroom rush, with the boss fights

- **Owner:** "the slow country theme shall slowly progress into metal themes, and turn into something like the Sabre Dance ... Mācron's little feet start moving quickly alternating up and down, with his tiny hands holding his crotch, as the music intensity goes up ... its cinematic exit shall be a rush stallroom open and toilet, then a zoom into Mācron's little eyes in a sign of relief".
- **Boss queue:** from the owner's earlier ruling, "defeat every previous opponent in their hardest dance combo to switch places in the bathroom queue". Each opponent's named hardest combo is already in their data.
- **Copyright:** Khachaturian's *Sabre Dance* (1942) needs a copyright check. He died in 1978, so it is very likely still under copyright in most countries. The design agent researches this. Most likely we write an original frenzied piece in that spirit (an ostinato xylophone and snare frenzy), never its melody.
- **Mācron's need:** it rises with the tiers, with feet alternating faster and hands held in front. It stays tasteful and nonviolent. The style guide already says "Restroom humour is a pictogram and relieved movement".

### 12. VIP: Tlatoāni, tacos al pastor, and the credits

- **The credits:** credits music plays while text scrolls up listing all the software packages used, headed "Built using Tillandsias.org", then Podman, Fedora 45, and the rest. Direct dependencies come first and larger; second-degree and deeper dependencies follow in more columns, in smaller text.
