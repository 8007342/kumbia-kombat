# Next level: El Salón de Espejos, with Don Terciopelo

**Design, 2026-11-07.** The owner asked for "a new mansion room" past the pool room, with "Bolero-cha elegance" for its music, and said: "You give it a charismatic name, define its style in the styleguide, make it charismatic like our other characters, and define its cinematic outro ;) then merge to game and open a local browser". So the names and choices below are the coordinator's. Each is a proposal the owner can reverse after playing it.

## The room

- **Id** `salon`. **Title** EL SALÓN DE ESPEJOS (the hall of mirrors). It comes after `billiards`, which hands over through a new bridge, `billiards_to_salon`. The salon becomes the last level, followed by the end screen.
- **What it is:** the mansion's grand ballroom, reached through double doors from the pool room. Gilded mirror panels line the walls between tall arched windows that look onto the night garden. The floor is polished herringbone parquet, and three crystal chandeliers give warm candle-gold light. Burgundy velvet drapes hang at the windows. A grand piano sits on a low dais at the far end, with a small trio (piano, violin, bongos). Couples in evening dress do a slow cha-cha around the edges.
- **Palette:** champagne gold, deep emerald, burgundy velvet, warm parquet brown, and near-black shadows. The mirrors repeat the chandeliers' glow, the way the billiards room uses its floor reflections and black ceiling for depth.
- **Lamp light:** candle-gold from the chandeliers, pooling on the parquet. The mirrors carry soft doubled highlights.
- **Starting point:** `tool/art/rooms/ballroom.json` and its 6 weak trial plates. Fork it to `tool/art/rooms/salon.json`.
- **Metric:** `Charm`.

## The opponent: Don Terciopelo

- **Name** Don Terciopelo ("Mr. Velvet"). He.
- **Who:** an older ballroom gentleman who has owned this floor for forty years. Tall, slim and very upright, with swept-back silver hair, a pencil moustache, a white dinner jacket, a burgundy velvet bow tie, a red rose in his lapel, black trousers and two-tone spectator shoes.
- **Silhouette:** a tall narrow vertical, the opposite of the stocky Gatekeeper and wide BlinBlin. The rose is his one red accent.
- **Charisma:** he dances with absurd poise: the cha-cha-chá triple step, hip lead and long arm lines. Between phrases he smooths his moustache, checks himself in a mirror, and offers a hand to invite Mācron onto the floor. He is never mean, only elegantly unimpressed, until he's won over.
- **Hardest combo**, for the later boss level: `EL GIRO DE TERCIOPELO`, a spin into a held line with a double-time cha-cha-chá.

## Music: bolero-cha

- **Score** `salon_bolero` "Terciopelo", with `salon_bolero_success` and the bridge `billiards_to_salon`.
- **Style:** a cha-cha-chá with a sung-style bolero melody carried by violin and flute, piano montuno, nylon guitar, güiro on the cha-cha-chá, congas, soft bass, and strings swelling at the top tier. Elegant, romantic, unhurried.
- **Tempos:** 108, 116 and 124 BPM. All three tiers come from one performance, the method that worked for the party and billiards. Success is 4 bars. A minor key that resolves to its relative major in the success.

## The cinematic outro

1. On the win, Don Terciopelo stops mid-turn, looks at Mācron in the mirror, then turns and gives him a deep, slow ballroom bow.
2. **Close-up** (section 8 of the style guide, the same pattern as the bar's oil gulp and the party's nod): he plucks the rose from his lapel and offers it. Mācron takes it and tucks it behind his antenna. The title **QUÉ / ELEGANCIA** sits on the handoff.
3. Back to the wide shot: the chandeliers flare softly and the dancing couples turn to applaud. Don Terciopelo sweeps an arm toward the far double doors, and Mācron walks out through them, rose on his antenna, into the end screen.

## Feedback from the owner's phone test, done in the same batch

Verbatim: "I get a lot of timeout taps, the threshold is too strict and punishing :( also, the later harder combos at the highest speed I only had to beat once or twice depending on the level, versus failing several times the easy part :P the red thingy with the eyelash is a late sign, right? and the golden star is on time? the blue arrow is early? We might need to highlight around the finger's tap instead of over the tap, those highlights are a little confusing. A asked a few players to try it and they weren't clear that it was a fight and tried to tap the opponent taps, we need to respond with some crosses to those taps, to let the user have some feedback when they're playing out of turn."

What the code shows:
- **Windows:** `RhythmJudge` uses ±75 ms for perfect and ±180 ms for good. Beyond that a tap is a miss, which shows as the timeout.
- **No latency compensation:** nothing offsets phone audio output latency (`crowd_interest.dart` notes that calibration isn't built). Many phones play sound 100 to 250 ms late, which uses up most of the 180 ms window before the player does anything.
- **Marks:** gold star means perfect, blue means early, ember means late. That matches the owner's reading.
