## 1. Timing: what changed

**Fact, from the code before this change.** Taps were judged on the Web Audio clock
(`AudioContext.currentTime`), at the frame that handled them. That is when the browser
hands sound to the device, not when the player hears it. A phone plays sound later than
that, and its touch screen reports a tap later than the thumb lands. Both delays make an
on-time player look late. The late window was 180 ms, and whatever the phone's delays used
up was gone before the player did anything.

**Fact, from research.** These come from RESEARCH's cheatsheet,
`plan/research/rhythm-music-engagement-2026-11-07.md`, merged into `game` at c611f43. The
cheatsheet tags its sources and marks some numbers "memory, verify".
- People tap slightly *ahead* of a beat they hear, by a few tens of ms. This is negative
  mean asynchrony (Repp 2005, Psychon Bull Rev 12:969; Repp & Su 2013, Psychon Bull Rev
  20:403). So "late" readings come from the pipeline, not from slow humans.
- Web Audio's `outputLatency` is "an estimation" (MDN). Chrome reports it, Safari reports
  only `baseLatency`, and some Androids report nonsense (Chromium issue 40103372).
  Bluetooth adds 150 to 300 ms (RTINGS). No window can absorb that; only calibration can.
- Touch screens measure 50 to 200 ms end to end (Deber et al., CHI 2015). People can't feel
  touch latency under about 24 ms (Jota et al., CHI 2013). Untrained tablet tappers are
  less consistent than in the lab, partly because touch screens sample at 60 to 240 Hz
  (Zagala, Foster & Dalla Bella 2021, Front Psychol 12:607676).
- Windows elsewhere: osu! at OD5 is 50/100/150 ms; Arcaea's Pure is 50 ms and Far is
  100 ms; StepMania's defaults top out at 180 ms; Rhythm Quest, a casual game, is about
  ±125 ms. Our 75/180 was already more lenient than all of them. Widening isn't the fix;
  centring is.
- Rhythm games shift *input judging* by a measured offset, never the audio (Guitar Hero,
  Rhythm Quest devlog 10, Rhythm Doctor).

**Changed** in `lib/gameplay/rhythm.dart`, `garden_session.dart`, `garden_page.dart`,
`sync_calibration.dart`, `sync_overlay.dart`, `sync_store*.dart`,
`lib/music/game_audio_contract.dart` and `web_audio_backend.dart`.

- **Judging on what was heard.** A tap is judged at:
  audio clock − output latency − touch delay − this device's calibration − auto-nudge,
  taken at the time of the touch event itself.
  - *Output latency* is `outputLatency + baseLatency` when the browser reports an output
    latency above 0. Otherwise it is `baseLatency`, and with neither it is 60 ms. It is
    capped at 0.5 s. Chrome reports 0 before the first sound, so the value is re-read
    between responses, never during one.
  - *Touch delay* is a 40 ms default (`defaultTouchLatencySeconds`).
  - *Taps are timed from the touch event, not the frame.* On the web, a pointer event's
    stamp is the DOM event's. Its age (`performance.now()` minus the stamp) comes off the
    tap. A stamp outside 0 to 150 ms is ignored, so a busy frame can't make a tap late.
  - *Response cues are drawn on the audible clock*, using output latency only, so a ring
    lands when its beat is heard. The touch delay, calibration and nudge affect judging
    only. The music is scheduled as before, and dancers animate on the audio clock.
  - AutoPlay plays on the judged clock, so it stays perfect.
- **Windows.**
  - Perfect stays ±75 ms.
  - Early stays 180 ms.
  - **Late goes from 180 to 200 ms** (`RhythmJudge.lateWindowMs`). This is the small late
    allowance the cheatsheet suggests while a player hasn't calibrated. Most won't
    calibrate, and browsers report latency roughly or not at all.
  - I first tried 230 ms, then cut it back once the research showed that centring is the
    fix.
  - The owner suspected "feeling 'on time' probably requires a bit of lag acceptance".
    The studies say people tap early, so the lag is the machine's. That is why the
    allowance is on the late side: it covers the estimate, not the player.
- **Tap-along calibration, from the title screen.** A **SYNC** button now sits beside
  CALM CAMERA.
  - It plays 4 clicks to listen to, then 16 to tap with, at 100 BPM.
  - You tap anywhere. Nothing on screen moves with the beat, so it measures by ear.
  - It takes the median offset of the taps, on top of the game's own estimate. Taps more
    than 150 ms from the median are dropped, and it needs at least 8 taps to trust.
  - The result is capped at ±250 ms and stored per device in the browser's storage. It
    shows as a sync slider (blue early, ember late), not as milliseconds.
  - It shifts judging only, never the music or the cues. The button then reads SYNCED,
    and FORGET SYNC clears it.
- **Auto-nudge** (a proposal, on only while the player hasn't calibrated). When a phrase
  ends, if the median of its accepted taps sat more than 40 ms off, judging moves by half
  of that. The total is capped at ±60 ms and carries across the run's rooms. It needs at
  least 4 taps in the phrase (`AutoNudge`).
- **Chart fairness stays as honest as it was.**
  - The chart validator now uses `RhythmJudge.guaranteedWindowMs` (180 ms, the old
    window) for its phrase-end check and its move-offer room. So no chart changed and no
    optional move disappeared.
  - The extra 20 ms of late window is best effort: a tap there can overlap an offered
    move, which only costs the move.
  - A phrase is decided by `cycleEnd − phraseEndMs` at the latest, so latency can't push
    the decision past the moment the next tier must be requested.
  - ALIGN and LEVEL were told these numbers.
- **Hidden `?latency=<ms>`** replaces the browser's report, the touch delay, the saved
  calibration and the nudge with one number, for example `?latency=150`. `?latency=0`
  turns compensation off.

## 2. Difficulty: what changed (proposal, reversible)

The owner said "the later harder combos at the highest speed I only had to beat once or
twice depending on the level, versus failing several times the easy part". The rules
agreed: most rooms asked for 2 peak passes (`finalChallenges`). Much of the early failing
was probably latency, which section 1 addresses, so these changes are small.

| level | earlyHitRatio (before the peak) | finalChallenges (peak passes) |
|---|---|---|
| garden | 0.75 (unchanged) | 2 → **3** |
| pool-garden | 0.75 (unchanged) | 2 → **3** |
| bar | 0.875 → **0.75** | 2 → **3** |
| cool-crowd | 0.875 → **0.75** | 3 (unchanged) |
| billiards | 0.875 → **0.75** | 2 → **3** |

- **Before the peak: 6 of 8 steps instead of 7.** A player still learning a room can drop
  two steps a phrase, as the lawn and the pool already allowed.
- **At the peak: 3 passes everywhere, each still 7 of 8 steps.** A failed peak phrase
  still drops back a tier and clears the count. So the win means three passed peak
  phrases with no fall between them. That proof is earned, and it adds about one phrase
  (6 to 8 s) to a clean run.
- Not changed: `successesPerTier`, `resetAfterFailures`, and the peak's 7 of 8.
- Proposed for the salon: the same rules, 0.75 before the peak and 3 peak passes.
- The crowd's arc reads `finalChallenges`, so a room's crowd now builds over one more
  phrase.

## 3. Tap marks

The owner read the marks right: the gold star is on time, the blue arrow is early, and
the ember tail is late. They found them confusing because they sat over the tap. Changed
in `lib/gameplay/tap_feedback.dart`:
- **A halo rings the touch point.** It is a full circle of radius 38 px in the grade's
  colour, just outside the pad of a thumb (about 40 to 55 px across). It opens a little as
  it fades. Nothing is drawn on the spot the thumb covers.
- **The grade mark sits 78 px above the touch.** It is the old ring with its star, chevron
  or tail. The thumb comes up from below, so the mark stays readable.
- Marks follow where the thumb actually landed, not the ring's centre. A key press has no
  touch point, so its marks use the ring's landing spot.

## 4. Out-of-turn taps

A tap while the challenger is dancing (the call) gets a slate **✕ cross** above it, with a
dull halo round it, for 0.45 s. It also gets a soft low knock: a 110 Hz pulse at low
velocity, at most one per 0.12 s.
- The tap is never judged. It isn't a miss, breaks no combo and costs nothing
  (`GardenSession.opponentsTurn`, `lastTapOutOfTurn`).
- The early grace before the first response note still counts as a real tap. That grace
  is the full 180 ms early window, on the judged clock. The cross shows only before it
  opens, and `latency_test.dart` checks both sides of that edge.

## What to try on the phone

1. Play the lawn without Bluetooth and without calibrating. Most taps should now read
   gold, with fewer late embers and timeouts. The auto-nudge should settle what's left
   within a few phrases.
2. On the title screen, press **SYNC**: listen to four clicks, then tap sixteen. Note
   where the slider lands, then play the lawn again. If you still see embers or blue
   arrows, tell us which, and on which phone and browser.
3. With Bluetooth headphones, calibrate with them on. Their 150 to 300 ms delay is beyond
   any window; only calibration covers it, up to 250 ms.
4. To compare by hand, `?latency=150` (or 60, or 200) sets everything to one number.
5. Tap the rings while the challenger dances. You should get a grey ✕ and a soft knock,
   and nothing should count against you.
6. Watch the marks around your thumb. The halo should ring it, and the star, arrow or
   tail should be readable above it.
7. Play any room to the end. The peak now takes three passes, and the tiers before it
   forgive two dropped steps.

## Not done, and risks

- The calibration clicks are synth pulses, not the room's song. Calibrating on the kick
  of the real music, as the cheatsheet suggests, would be closer to play.
- Not checked in a real browser:
  - that Flutter web's pointer stamps share `performance.now()`'s clock. If they don't,
    the plausibility check drops them and taps are timed at the frame, as before.
  - the calibration's local storage. In a private window it falls back to this run
    only.
- Nothing logs the signed offsets for us to read later. Only the auto-nudge uses them,
  in play.
- None of these numbers has met a real thumb, so the owner's test decides.
